# Editing tasks, data sources, and industry evaluations

## Existing work worth drawing on

The literature here supports task design; it does not constitute a validated "de-AI-ese" dataset. The literature search is current through 2026-09-12; these datasets have not been downloaded or redistributed.

| Work | Original evidence and scope | Use for the project, and limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Raheja et al., 2023, **CoEdIT: Text Editing by Task-Specific Instruction Tuning** | The paper trains on 82K editing instructions; [the public train split is about 69K](https://github.com/vipulraheja/coedit), with some samples withheld for licensing reasons. It covers simplification, tone, and other editing tasks. The paper explicitly acknowledges that it mainly covers sentence-level editing, that its effect on long texts remains to be verified, and that the uniform prompt format does not adequately control for each model's prompt sensitivity. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.350/) | Supports training "input text + editing requirement → revised draft". Its historical baseline results cannot be used to predict how today's strong models will fare on long-form bilingual Chinese-English text. PDF methods, experiments, and Limitations read. |
| Du et al., 2022, **Understanding Iterative Revision from Human-Written Text** | IteraTeR collected about 31K document revisions from Wikipedia, arXiv, and Wikinews, and annotated the edit intent. Later versions differ in size, so the version should be pinned. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.250/), [author code](https://github.com/vipulraheja/iterater) | Draw on its edit intent and multi-granularity revision; it is not ready-made gold for marketing copy, API documentation, or mixed Chinese-English writing. A later version is also not necessarily better than an earlier one on every dimension. Methods, corpus statistics, and the authors' README read. |
| Rao & Tetreault, 2018, **Dear Sir or Madam, May I Introduce the GYAFC Dataset: Corpus, Benchmarks and Metrics for Formality Style Transfer** | Builds formal/informal parallel rewrite data from Yahoo Answers. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/N18-1012/) | Useful for learning that several rewrites can be legitimate, and for evaluating meaning preservation separately; formality is not naturalness, and informal text must not be treated as the positive class. Only the official abstract and metadata were checked; the full agreement and data terms must be read before any real use. |
| Wu et al., 2025, **WritingBench: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Generative Writing** | v4: 1,000 tasks, 445 Chinese and 555 English, 6 major domains and 100 subdomains; dynamic per-task rubric. Human evaluation of its 300 separate tasks is used to check the judge. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05244v4) | Worth drawing on for "task-relevant evaluation". It studies generative writing, not the naturalness of a rewrite. Its writer is trained on synthetic data filtered by the critic, and the related evaluation stays inside the same evaluation system, so an independent human final test must be added. §§3–4 and Appendix D read. |
| Bhat, Browne & Bingemann, 2025, **Creativity Benchmark: A benchmark for marketing creativity for LLM models** | 678 industry practitioners made 11,012 anonymous pairwise comparisons; 100 brands; the tasks were insights, ideas, and wild ideas. Agreement between the LLM judge and expert rankings was weak and varied with the judge. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09702v1) | For marketing creativity, a general-purpose judge cannot simply be carried over. Volunteer participation, English-market demographic skew, short creative tasks, and the decoding settings all limit extrapolation; it also does not measure sales conversion. §§4–7 and 9 read. |
| Liu et al., 2025, **LLMs for Customized Marketing Content Generation and Evaluation at Scale** | MarketingFM uses product/search context, task chaining, and rule-plus-model review, and reports offline review and an online advertising experiment. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17863v1) | A strong harness should include real source material; otherwise the training group gets more facts and the comparison becomes unfair. An improvement in ad relevance cannot be read directly as an improvement in naturalness. Abstract, architecture, experiments, and the human evaluation section read. The "Received 2009" in the HTML is an anomalous template field; this entry uses the arXiv v1 version date of 2025-06-22. |
| Lin & Ma, 2024, **Generating Attractive and Authentic Copywriting from Customer Reviews** | Uses the experiences found in reviews to generate product copy, while evaluating both attractiveness and faithfulness. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.259/) | A reminder that "more specific" often depends on better input evidence. Experiences reported in reviews cannot be automatically generalized into product guarantees. Only the official abstract has been checked so far; kept as a candidate for full reading later. |
| **MCTS: A Multi-Reference Chinese Text Simplification Dataset**, LREC-COLING 2024 | A Chinese multi-reference simplification dataset. [Paper and author metadata](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.969/), [author repository](https://github.com/blcuicall/mcts) | Multiple references are well suited to validating that "there can be more than one good answer"; a simplification objective cannot substitute for technical accuracy or brand voice. Only the official abstract/project description was checked; it has not been confirmed as directly usable for the target task. |
| Wang et al., 2022, **CCTC: A Cross-Sentence Chinese Text Correction Dataset for Native Speakers** | Cross-sentence error correction aimed at native speakers; the official abstract notes that native speakers and learners have different error distributions. [Paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.coling-1.294/) | Used as related work on Chinese error correction; grammatical correctness cannot be taken as the absence of AI-ese. Only the official abstract was checked. |

## Ground truth should be split into four kinds of material

**Source anchors.** Text from before 2022-11-30 that carries date evidence helps lower the chance of ChatGPT-assisted writing mixing in; a date cannot prove that a text was written entirely by a human, nor can it prove quality. Retain the original publication date, version evidence, author/brand, source, and usage rights. An old date shown on a web page today may conceal a later rewrite, so prefer a commit or an archival snapshot whose version can be pinned.

**Quality anchors.** Text that native-speaker domain editors evaluate, revise, and explain from the same brief and the same factual material. Parts of writing quality are genuinely arguable, so the labels should preserve disagreement and different acceptable versions. Do not force a single "standard answer."

**Controlled comparisons.** Under the same fact packet, audience, channel, and length constraints, collect a human draft, multi-model drafts, strong-prompt drafts, and mixed-edit drafts separately. Historical work cannot be guaranteed to yield a reconstruction of its real brief, so such reconstructed samples should be labeled separately to avoid mixing them with forward-looking tasks.

**Counterfactual minimal pairs.** Change one phenomenon at a time: hollow contrasts, deleting a key condition, fabricated numbers, excessive headings, replacing terminology with synonyms, legitimate contrasts, necessary repetition. Minimal pairs are used to check whether the judge understands function; the main test still uses real, complete documents, so that it does not merely learn to solve artificial exercises.

## Data acquisition priority

| Source | Acquisition Method | Quality and Usage Rights Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Newly commissioned Chinese and English domain editors | Write from scratch or revise a draft around the same evidence packet | Explicitly record AI assistance, the editing rationale, and training and redistribution permissions; this is the gold candidate closest to the target |
| Owned and licensed legacy copy, legacy documents, and version diffs | Imported as collections by author/brand/project | Company material is not accessed automatically; before any real adoption, decide separately which content may enter the personal repository and the training set |
| Historical documentation in open-source projects | Choose documentation paths with a clear license, and pin the commit and the date | A documentation license is not necessarily the same as the code license; record each source's terms of use, and review revision quality |
| Historical marketing sites / ad archives | Build an inventory of links and metadata first | "Publicly readable" and "long ago" do not amount to permission to train or redistribute; this round does not copy full text for ingestion |
| Public editing/simplification datasets | Select small samples according to task purpose, and verify versions and terms | Used as auxiliary training or as a sanity check; they are not named as a validated de-AI-ese dataset |

## Data shortcuts to avoid

Randomly splitting train/test by paragraph leaks author style, facts from the same document, and brand language. Form collections by source family first, then split, then generate variants; translations, drafts of the same topic from different models, a single campaign, and adjacent versions must stay in the same collection. Publicly available historical material may also appear in base pretraining, so the test set needs a sizeable proportion of newly commissioned material.

Synthesizing "bad drafts" from good human drafts and then training the reverse recovery can provide cheap local supervision, but it easily picks up the habits of the particular corruptor. Such data must be labeled separately and kept to a limited share, and the final test should use real models' native defects and real user drafts. Give every model the same fact packet, so that the model group does not lose to the original author merely for want of material.

Chinese and English must each be written natively and annotated natively; translation pairs serve only as an extra consistency test. Chinese should not be penalized for using parallelism, and English should not be penalized for a formal tone or non-native phrasing. Every "AI feature" needs functional evidence in context.
