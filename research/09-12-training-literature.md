# Plainvoice: Training Methods, Dock Size, and Prompting Comparison

Implementation guidelines: Designs numbered B0–B5 in this memorandum, and the learning curves from 400 to 1,600 to 4,000 are alternative designs; a unified experimental arm and phased samples will be adopted.[Main scheme](../09-12-research-and-experiment-plan.md)The experiment was not actually started.

Research date: 2026-09-12 (America/Los_Angeles). This document provides research and experimental recommendations; no model download, computing power purchase, training data creation, or training initiation is required. Chinese, English, technical documents, and marketing documents are given equal priority. Model availability, structure, and license are from the official card; its general benchmark score does not reflect its performance on this task.

It is recommended to initially define the task as **constrained editing**: while maintaining facts, argument relationships, purpose, and authorial voice, reduce vagueness, mechanical elaboration, and unnecessary rhetoric. The first round of training should use a 7–9B instruction/post-trained framework for SFT + LoRA/QLoRA, while retaining a 4B cost reference and a 27B cue word reference. Only if SFT still exhibits stable and annotable style preference issues should a small-scale DPO be added. Currently, there is no evidence to support direct implementation in online RL, nor is there evidence that a single parameter size is sufficient.

## 1. The training hypothesis that most needs to be modified in the original plan

**High-quality human articles are suitable for providing style references, but not for directly constituting rewriting training pairs.** Pretraining with only next-token continued text on old articles teaches the continuation distribution of that corpus; it doesn't teach the model how to retain numbers, constraints, API names, and arguments based on the input. Packaging articles as arbitrary reverse-engineered prompts may also create training samples where the input lacks facts but the output does. The model might learn to "write more specifically" to fill in missing details. This is a risk derived from the task definition, not a failure rate measured in a particular paper.

Priority should be given to collecting `原稿 + 使用场景/受众 + 不可改事实/结构约束 → 编辑后稿` Record the reasons for editing. Allow real people to edit original drafts of real models, lengthy drafts by real people, and already good drafts; allow the latter to remain unchanged or make only minor changes. There's no need to label all drafts as AI: the product should address specific writing flaws.

Transforming human masterpieces into AI-generated versions and then restoring them can serve as supplementary synthetic data, but should not be used as the primary test set. This can easily lead models to learn only the reversal of a particular generator's contamination rules; the original masterpiece may also contain facts never provided in the generation prompt. The same original text and all its contaminations, rewrites, translations, and candidate drafts must belong to the same data split.

Technical documentation should typically retain steps, preconditions, warnings, code, and precise terminology; marketing can retain effective contrasts, pacing, brand tone, and emotion. "Not X, but Y" is not an automatic negative example: the problem is whether it explains a genuine distinction. Using sentence structure disqualification rules as training objectives will yield another set of templates.

## 2. Papers directly related to decision-making

|---|---|---|
| [LoRA — Hu et al., 2021-06-17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685) | Freezing the master model and learning parameter updates through a low-rank matrix is an efficient method for fine-tuning parameters. | LoRA is not a learning objective on par with SFT/DPO; SFT or DPO can be implemented using LoRA. Using an adapter first facilitates comparison and rollback; the paper does not prove that low-rank updates are sufficient to eliminate all style problems across four layers.

| [Direct Preference Optimization — Rafailov et al., 2023-05-29; revised 2024-07-29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290) | It directly optimizes preferences using preferred/rejected answers to the same input, without requiring a separate explicit reward model or rollout in standard offline training. Experiments were conducted on sentiment, summary, and dialogue. | It is suitable for handling pairwise preferences where "both are correct, but which one sounds more natural." HyPO, using online samples for KL regularization, is proposed and tested on abstract and general chat. DPO should not be considered completely equivalent to all online RL. A more practical initial approach is to sample from the current SFT model, label the preferences, and then perform another round of offline training; this is an engineering suggestion for this project, not a direct validation of this workflow in the paper.
| [Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization in Direct Alignment Algorithms — Rafailov et al., 2024-06-05; revised 2024-11-05](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02900v2) | DPO/IPO/SLiC can also be over-optimized, and performance can decrease as optimization progresses. These papers provide methods and failure modes, but cannot replace dedicated task verification. In particular, the conclusions of abstracts/dialogues cannot be directly regarded as rewritten conclusions of technical documents or marketing materials; the judgment of native Chinese style cannot be inferred from English results.

## 3. Which prompting/harness baselines should be established first?

Compare the following configurations under **the same base, the same frozen test input, and the same fact constraints**. Additionally, add a strong commercial model as a product alternative; cross-model results cannot be attributed to "training is better than prompting".

| Configuration | Experiment Content | Questions to be Answered |
|---|---|---|
| B0 Return as is | No rewriting; retain all original drafts | Did the model worsen a good draft? Was the rewriting truly valuable?
| B1 Simple Prompt | Clearly state the purpose, target audience, and factual constraints; minimize redundancy and maintain a consistent tone. | What can the product achieve at its lowest cost?
| B2 Task: Rubric + Few-Shot | Provides several authorized before-and-after edit examples for each language/scenario; includes sentence structures that should be retained and unmodified examples. | Can writing preferences be expressed with a few specific examples?
| B3 Search Examples | Only use edit pairs with the same language, genre, and defect type from the training split as examples | Is a controlled sample library more effective than a fixed prompt? Test manuscripts or near-duplicate versions should not be searched. |
| B4 Location/Rewrite/Verification | First, identify redundant or false comparison points, then make limited modifications, and finally verify the facts against the code; at most one round of repair | Do the multiple steps result in sufficient improvement? Pay special attention to factual loss and p95 delay.
| B5 Best-of-N with the same budget | For example, generating two candidates, selected by an independent evaluator, with a total token budget close to B4 | Does B4's gain come from the revision strategy, or simply from using more inference resources?
| T1 SFT adapter | Same base, using edit pairs reviewed by real people, aiming for single-output as much as possible | Is it possible to compress repeatable prompt capabilities into a cheaper and more stable model behavior?
| T2 SFT + DPO adapter | Generates candidates with the same input from T1/current policy; all are labeled as preferences after passing the fact threshold | Does preference training bring gains beyond SFT?

The prompt can only be adjusted after the training/development/testing partition is fixed. For each output record, specify the base revision, template, thinking settings, quantization method, sampling seed, input/output/thinking token, wall time, and failure and retries. First compare matched compute, then provide the quality/cost curves for the actual deployment of each method; do not simply compare style scores from one SFT call versus three API calls.

The validator should have at least two layers: deterministic diffing should be performed on numbers, links, references, code, APIs/flags, etc.; semantic changes such as causality, conditionality, scope, probability/determinism should be handled by independent review and random sampling verification. Fact checking is not a general embedding similarity score. Technical documentation can be set to directly disqualify key semantic errors; marketing must not use fabricated customer cases, data, or exaggerated promises to gain a sense of concreteness.

## 5. Phased training design

These quantities are the starting range for estimating workload, not the data thresholds proven by the literature; the final determination is based on the learning curve and the consistency of annotations.

1. **First, calibrate the task and baseline.** Build a four-layered, balanced, and source-isolated experimental set; each layer should contain at least well-written drafts, genuinely poor drafts, key technical/business constraints, and a range of lengths. Perform blind human reviews for B0–B5, allowing for tie-ins; record the reasons, rather than simply asking "Does it resemble AI?" If B2/B4 already meet product requirements, the value of training is mainly to reduce invocation costs, latency, or deployment dependencies.
2. **SFT:** Learn the curve with a small sample size. Human review of 400 → 1,600 → 4,000 edit pairs, with each of the four layers accounting for 25%; frozen test data cannot be reused. The training objective only covers the formal rewriting of the assistant, without training the model to output packaging such as "The following is a de-AI-featured version." No/minimally modified cases are added to the training set; the specific proportion is determined by the over-edit rate. Pre-labeling ensures the facts are preserved; rejection is achieved by removing constraints to obtain a smooth target.
3. **Efficient parameter implementation.** LoRA is used initially; QLoRA is used if devices are insufficient. Candidate rank is 16/32, with a smaller learning-rate grid and a starting time of 1–2 epochs. Checkpoints are selected based on the development set. Numerical values represent hypotheses to be tuned. Modern hybrid attention architectures cannot blindly reuse old models. `q_proj/v_proj` List; adapter overrides are determined by the actual module name. Only text paths are trained; whether to unload/freeze other components requires verification of framework support.
4. **DPO: Resolves only remaining preferences.** Multiple candidates are generated from the current model for the same original text, and two versions that pass fact-checking are manually compared. Language, genre, defect type, and length difference labels are preserved; challenging examples such as "shorter but missing facts," "more lively but overly colloquial," and "preserving appropriate contrasts is better than abrupt disabling" are emphasized. It can start with 1,000–3,000 preference pairs; evaluation should also be performed early in training, and the last checkpoint should not be saved alone. A small ablation process can be performed between direct DPO and SFT→DPO, but SFT-only should not be omitted.
5. **Online learning should be considered later.** Sampling, calibration, and iterative DPO should only be considered if the fixed preference set coverage is insufficient, new generator styles emerge, or the calibrated evaluator remains reliable on new data. Online PPO/GRPO should only be initiated if the benefits of this process are still limited by exploration, and if there is sufficient traffic and funding. The hard constraint is the candidate threshold; style is merely a ranking factor after passing the threshold. A single objective of "higher reward for lower AI detector scores" should not be adopted.

If one candidate is more natural but contains a key factual error, it should not be used as the overall preferred sample; factual priority should be explicitly stated, or the comparison should be labeled as a separate style dimension to avoid confusion with the overall preference. The training objective of DPO does not automatically know "fidelity priority".

## 7. Unknown items that must be retained

- There are currently no training results on the four layers of this project; it cannot be asserted that post-training is superior to strong prompt words, nor can the quality order of 4B/9B/27B be asserted.
- There is no evidence that a uniform style of reward is compatible with the structural requirements of technical documents and the brand voice of marketing; results and failure categories at each level should be reported, and macro averages should not mask regressions in Chinese or technical documentation.
- General writing benchmarks, official model-card scores, and LLM judge win rates can be used for screening, but cannot be used as alternative proofs of conversion rates for business copy or executability of technical documents.
- The QLoRA memory results in the paper come from different epochs; the training support for the new model's hybrid attention and multimodal paths must be tested in practice.
- If inconsistencies in manual annotations stem from genuine aesthetic differences, conditional preferences, style profiles, and tie should be preserved, and they should not be forcibly compressed into a uniform gold scalar.
- Simply shortening the text, removing the title, avoiding high-frequency words, or adding colloquialisms or errors might make some judges think it's more human-like; it requires simultaneous verification from three aspects: "information retention + writing quality + effectiveness in the usage scenario".

The next step should be to produce a rubric that can be discussed, four-layered samples, and a frozen baseline protocol; the training configuration is simply an implementation choice after these definitions are stabilized.
